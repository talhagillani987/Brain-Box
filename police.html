Police Rules and Laws in Pakistan

The Police force in Pakistan is responsible for maintaining law and order, protecting citizens, preventing crime, and enforcing the country's laws. The police system works under specific rules and legal frameworks set by the government.

Main Laws Governing Police in Pakistan

Police Order 2002 – The main law that organizes and regulates the police force in Pakistan, defining their powers, duties, and structure.

Pakistan Penal Code (PPC) 1860 – Defines crimes and their punishments; police use this to identify offences.

Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) 1898 – Explains how police should investigate crimes, arrest people, and present cases in court.

Police Rules 1934 – Older set of detailed rules about police duties, conduct, and administration (still referenced in many areas).

Structure of Police in Pakistan

Inspector General of Police (IGP) – Head of police in a province.

Deputy Inspector General (DIG) – Manages a region/division.

Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) – Head of a district.

Station House Officer (SHO) –In-charge of a local police station.

Constables –Basic-rank officers who patrol and assist in daily duties.

Main Duties of Police

Preventing Crime – Patrolling areas to stop crimes before they happen.

Investigating Crimes – Collecting evidence, questioning suspects, and solving cases.

Arresting Criminals – Taking lawbreakers into custody following legal procedure.

Filing FIR (First Information Report) – Officially recording a complaint or crime report.

Maintaining Public Order – Managing crowds, protests, and public events peacefully.

Protecting Citizens' Rights – Ensuring people's safety and legal rights are respected.

Assisting Courts – Presenting evidence, arrested individuals, and reports in legal proceedings.

Powers of Police

Can arrest a person suspected of a crime (with or without a warrant, depending on the offence).

Can search a place with proper legal permission (search warrant).

Can investigate and collect evidence for a case.

Can produce an arrested person before a magistrate within 24 hours (as per law)

Important Rights of Citizens When Dealing with Police

Right to know the reason for arrest.

Right to legal representation (a lawyer).

Right to be presented before a court within 24 hours of arrest.

Right to file a complaint against police misconduct.

Common Terms Related to Police & Law

FIR (First Information Report): Initial complaint document that starts a criminal case.

Bail:Temporary release of an accused person before trial.

Warrant: Legal document allowing police to arrest someone or search a property.

Custody: When police legally hold a suspect.
