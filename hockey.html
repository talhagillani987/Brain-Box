Hockey Information And Records

What is Hockey

Field hockey is a popular team sport played between two teams of 11 players each. The objective of the game is to score more goals than the opposing team by hitting a small hard ball into the opponent's goal using a curved hockey stick. Only the goalkeeper is allowed to use any part of the body to stop the ball inside the shooting circle, while field players must use only the flat side of their sticks. Hockey is a fast-paced game that requires speed, teamwork, skill, passing, dribbling, and accurate shooting. The team that scores the most goals by the end of the match is declared the winner.

history of Hockey

Field hockey has a long history that dates back thousands of years, with games similar to hockey being played in ancient Egypt, Greece, and Persia. However, the modern game of hockey was developed in England during the 19th century. The first official hockey club was formed in 1861, and the rules of the game were standardized a few years later. Hockey spread rapidly to many countries through the British Empire. Today, it is played professionally around the world and is governed internationally by the International Hockey Federation.

Rules of Hockey

A hockey match is played between two teams of 11 players, including one goalkeeper. The match consists of four quarters of 15 minutes each, making a total playing time of 60 minutes. Players move the ball using only the flat side of their hockey sticks and must not intentionally use their feet or body to control the ball. A goal is counted only if the ball is hit from inside the shooting circle. Dangerous play, obstruction, and stick fouls are not allowed. The team with the highest number of goals at the end of the match wins.

Hockey Field

The hockey field is a rectangular grass or artificial turf surface measuring 91.4 meters long and 55 meters wide. Each end of the field has a goal, and in front of each goal is a shooting circle, also called the D, from where goals must be scored. The field also includes a halfway line, 23-meter lines, side lines, back lines, and a penalty spot used for penalty strokes.

Number of Players

Each hockey team consists of 11 players on the field: 1 Goalkeeper 10 Field Players Teams are also allowed substitute players, and substitutions can be made throughout the match according to the competition rules.

Player Position

The players are divided into different positions based on their responsibilities:

Goalkeeper: Protects the goal and stops shots.

Defenders: Prevent the opposing team from scoring.

Midfielders: Link defense and attack by passing and controlling the game.

Forwards: Focus on creating scoring opportunities and scoring goals.

Each position is important for maintaining balance and teamwork during the match.

Hockey Equipment

The basic equipment used in hockey includes a hockey stick, a hard plastic ball, shin guards, mouth guard, sports shoes, and a team uniform. Goalkeepers wear additional protective equipment, including a helmet, chest protector, leg guards, kickers, gloves, and protective padding to prevent injuries.

Penalty Corner

A penalty corner is awarded when the defending team commits certain fouls inside their own shooting circle or intentionally commits a foul in the defensive area. During a penalty corner, attackers have an excellent chance to score while defenders must follow specific positioning rules until the ball is played.

Penalty Stroke

A penalty stroke is awarded when a defender illegally prevents a certain goal or commits a serious foul inside the shooting circle. During the penalty stroke, one attacker takes a direct shot against only the goalkeeper from the penalty spot. It is one of the best scoring opportunities in hockey.

Cards in Hockey

Hockey uses three types of cards to punish players:

Green Card: A warning and a short suspension.

Yellow Card: A temporary suspension for a longer period decided by the umpire.

Red Card: The player is permanently sent off and cannot return to the match.

These cards help maintain discipline and fair play.
