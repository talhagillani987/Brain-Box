Cricket information and Records

Cricket is a bat-and-ball sport played between two teams of 11 players on an oval-shaped field. The primary goal is to score the most runs. One team bats while the other bowls and fields; the teams then switch roles, and the team with the highest total score wins.

Cricket Wicket Rules

Bowled: The bowler delivers the ball and it hits the stumps directly, dislodging at least one bail.

Caught: A fielder (including the wicketkeeper) catches the ball cleanly after it has touched the striker’s bat or glove, but before it touches the ground.

Leg Before Wicket (LBW): The ball hits the batter's body (pad, leg, torso) instead of the bat. The umpire will give the batter out if the ball pitched in line or outside off-stump, would have gone on to hit the stumps, and wasn't struck outside the off-stump line.

Run Out: A fielder breaks the stumps with the ball in hand while the batter is out of their crease (safe zone) while attempting or completing a run.

Stumped: The wicketkeeper dislodges the bails with the ball in hand while the batter has stepped out of their crease to play a shot, but is not attempting a run.

Dismissal in Cricket

Hit Wicket: The batter accidentally dislodges their own bails with their bat or body while trying to play a shot or starting a run.

Timed Out: An incoming batter fails to be ready to face the next delivery within the set time limit (90 seconds in T20Is, 2 minutes in Tests and ODIs) from the fall of the previous wicket.

Hit the Ball Twice: The batter intentionally strikes the ball a second time with their bat or body after the initial legal stroke (other than to deflect it back to protect their wicket).

Obstructing the Field: The batter intentionally hinders or distracts the fielding team's efforts to catch or throw the ball.

Retired Out: A batter leaves the field without the umpire’s permission during the innings and does not return.
